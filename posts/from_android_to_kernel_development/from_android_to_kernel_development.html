from android to kernel development

April 12th, 2025

Below is a picture of a young mau visiting the google campus for the first time, just a few months after starting both android development and my overal computer science journey.

Android development was where I got to challenge myself in complex computer topics. It has led to me getting my first internship interview (although I failed miserably LOL), and has led me to really appreciate well thouhgtout architecture in software.

Despite android giving me some wonderful moments as well as giving me a chnage to really put my knowledge to the test with my recent project, I've decided that it's time to move on and leave android development behind.

Unfortunately, like most engineers, time is very limited. Ideally I'd love to be able to tinker on every single peice of tech that catches my interest. (and have done so in the past couple months) but it's clear that it's time to move on to what I've wanted to work on since picking up android development itself, that being low-level systems programming within the linux kernel.

This decision has come after a long battle of juggling both sides and while I enjoy both but as the topics witin the kernel become more and more complex it has required me to commit more time to it. Since the beginning I've wanted to have a really deep understanding of computers and getting to play with the kernel would get me closer to that level of engineering.

With this sudden shift I'll be open sourcing both my realtime tour guide app along with the backend code that supports it. While I don't intend to continue wokring on it idally I'd like to get PRs opened from those interested in android development. I opened my first open-source PR on the firefox android repo and would like to provide feedback those interested. Just like I was close to 5 years ago. (:

my final thoughts

I write this post on my way back to palm springs, california from an IBM offsite in austin, texas. I found myself getting emotional whilst I reflected on my time since installing android studio back in 2020 to now.

The thought of being able to get situated in kernel development where I'm currently learning about process management and process scheduling with the ability to explain it in a simplified manner is truly something that to me seemed so far to grasp a hold of 4.5 years ago. It has really been an amaziny journey and it's hard to believe I just graduated two years ago.

I hope this finds someone who has the same ambitions as me who is currently experiencing doubt.
